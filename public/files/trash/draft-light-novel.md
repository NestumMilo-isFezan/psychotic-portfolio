## Prologue: 3AM, Energy Drinks, and a Game That Should Not Exist

Kazuya Mishiro was, by all objective measures, a complete waste of human potential.

Twenty-three years old. Unwashed hoodie. Seventeen empty cans of _Chaos Energy MAX_ arranged in a perfect defensive perimeter around his gaming chair. His room smelled like ambition that had curdled into something more honest.

He had not left the apartment in eleven days.

This was fine. This was _optimal_. The outside world was a poorly designed game with no save points and terrible loot. He had no patience for it.

What he _did_ have patience for was **ELDEN ROSÉ: Villainess of Ruin** — the impossible crossover that the internet had unanimously declared "could not possibly be real." An otome visual novel with full soulslike combat. Multiple heroine routes. Punishing difficulty. A fanbase that spoke in hushed, traumatized tones.

He had bought it as a joke.

He was on hour two hundred and forty-seven.

> "System message: You have unlocked the True Chaos Ending prerequisites. There is no going back. Are you sure?"

Kazuya cracked open energy drink number eighteen.

"Obviously yes," he said to no one.

The screen went white.

Then he was not in his room anymore.

## Chapter 1: Mob Character #7, Reporting for Duty

The first thing he noticed was that the sky was the wrong color.

Purple. Not a nice purple. A _threatening_ purple, like the sky had made several poor life decisions and was now daring you to comment on it.

The second thing he noticed was the girl standing over him with a sword.

She had silver hair, violet eyes, and the particular expression of someone who had been planning an elegant soliloquy and was annoyed to find the dramatic moment interrupted by an unconscious man appearing from nowhere.

"...You," she said.

"Me," Kazuya agreed, still processing the sky situation.

"You are _Mob Character Seven_," she said. The capital letters were audible. "The background peasant who exists to be cut down in my route's inciting incident."

Kazuya sat up slowly. He recognized her immediately. **Seraphine Voss** — the primary villainess of ELDEN ROSÉ. Ice-type. Tragic backstory involving a cursed bloodline. Four possible endings depending on the player's karma score, ranging from "redemption arc" to "she personally executes you while crying."

He had gotten all four endings. Twice.

"Okay," he said. "So theoretically, what happens if I don't get cut down?"

Seraphine's sword arm lowered, one millimeter. "...That is not in the script."

"I know. I'm asking anyway."

She stared at him for a very long time.

"I have absolutely no idea," she finally admitted.

## Chapter 2: The Harem Begins (Against Everyone's Wishes, Including His)

The problem with being a mob character in a world with game logic was that the game logic did not _stop_ running just because you were anomalous.

By the end of the first week, Kazuya had accidentally:

- Saved Seraphine from an assassination attempt by tripping over a cobblestone at the exact right moment
- Tutored **Lira Ashveil** (the bookworm route's heroine) in "otherworld combat philosophy," which was just him describing Dark Souls lore with alarming intensity
- Become the first person in recorded history to make **Duchess Morrigan Vayle** (the villainess-route villainess, the _other_ one, more evil) laugh
- Gotten into a staring contest with **Yuki** (the silent shrine maiden heroine) and won, which apparently constituted a marriage proposal in her culture

None of this was intentional.

All of it had raised his affection flags with four different women simultaneously.

The game, operating on its original logic, was trying very hard to reconcile this with its existing route structures. It was failing. Error messages that only Kazuya could see flickered at the corners of his vision:

> _[ROUTE CONFLICT: Cannot process simultaneous S-rank affection from Heroine_01, Heroine_02, Villain_01, Villain_02. Running contingency protocol: CHAOS_MODE.]_

"Great," he muttered.

"You're talking to the air again," Lira said, not looking up from her book.

"I do that."

"I find it endearing." She turned a page. "Inexplicably."

## Chapter 3: Normal Harem Chaos (The Good Part)

The months that followed were, despite everything, kind of wonderful.

**Seraphine** thawed at approximately the speed of continental drift, which meant by month two she would occasionally stand next to him voluntarily and by month three she had told him her actual name rather than her title, which in villainess culture was apparently equivalent to a blood oath.

She was vicious, brilliant, and deeply funny in a way she absolutely refused to acknowledge. She had read three hundred books on tactical combat and could recite the weaknesses of every known monster type. She also kept a secret journal of pressed flowers that she would rather die than let anyone see.

Kazuya had seen it by accident. She had not killed him. This remained statistically remarkable.

**Lira** was the dangerous kind of gentle — the sort of person who would smile sweetly while quietly doing something completely unhinged. She had, over the course of four months, compiled a twelve-volume annotated analysis of everything Kazuya had said about soulslike game design, concluded that it was a form of applied metaphysics, and begun restructuring the kingdom's magical theory accordingly.

He had caused an academic revolution by rambling.

**Duchess Morrigan** was chaos in human form and she knew it and she approved of it. She had taken to appearing at inconvenient moments to offer Kazuya poisons "just to see if he could identify them by smell" and to discuss moral philosophy in ways that were technically correct but deeply unsettling. She had never once tried to kill him, which she maintained was her highest form of respect.

**Yuki** communicated primarily through extremely long meaningful silences and occasional handmade gifts. She had made him seven pairs of socks. They were very good socks. He suspected they were enchanted. He did not ask.

The game kept generating error messages. Kazuya kept ignoring them.

> _[WARNING: CHAOS_MODE threshold at 67%. Narrative coherence degrading. Heroine stability: fluctuating.]_

He closed the notification.

## Chapter 4: The Part Where Things Get Complicated

The problem was the _ending_.

Kazuya understood soulslike games. He understood that the beautiful, terrible thing about them was that they didn't care about your feelings. The story would go where the story went. The boss would kill you twenty times and not feel bad about it. The ending would be what it was whether you were ready or not.

He was a mob character in an otome soulslike.

He had known, in the abstract, what that meant.

It was around month five that he started noticing the signs. The way the sky went that specific shade of purple more often. The way Seraphine would sometimes look at him with an expression she immediately locked away. The way Lira's research had started focusing on curse reversal. The way Morrigan had stopped offering him poisons and started looking at him with something that was almost, almost worry.

The way Yuki had made him twelve pairs of socks now.

> _[CHAOS_MODE threshold at 89%. Heroine convergence imminent. Recommend immediate route resolution.]_

"Route resolution," Kazuya said quietly.

In the game, that was a technical term.

It meant: _someone dies._

In a harem route where all heroines had maxed affection scores and no clear winner, in a game running on soulslike logic—

He understood the ending now.

He didn't tell any of them.

## Chapter 5: The Last Good Day

They had a picnic. Lira's idea. She had found a hill outside the city that caught the light at exactly the right angle in the late afternoon, and she had insisted.

Morrigan complained the entire way there and then arranged herself decoratively on the grass and looked at the view with an expression she would deny was peaceful.

Seraphine sat next to Kazuya and did not mention the pressed flower she had tucked into her pocket that morning, a small white thing he pretended not to see.

Yuki sat on his other side and handed him a piece of rice cake and then watched him eat it with an expression of profound satisfaction, like she had accomplished something important.

Lira read aloud from a book about the philosophical implications of player agency in predetermined narratives, which was technically about him, and which she also maintained was not about him, and which everyone understood was completely about him.

The sky was the wrong purple.

It was the most right the sky had ever looked.

> _[CHAOS_MODE threshold: 99%. Final convergence: IMMINENT.]_

Kazuya looked at the four of them.

He thought: _if this is the end of the good part, at least the good part was good._

"Hey," he said.

They all looked at him.

"Thanks. For the... all of it."

Seraphine made a small, controlled sound. Morrigan looked away. Lira closed her book. Yuki handed him another piece of rice cake.

He ate it.

---

## Chapter 6: Convergence Protocol

It started at midnight.

Not dramatically. Just — he woke up and knew something had changed. The error messages were gone. The game's UI had vanished entirely. There was only the room, and the dark, and then four different sets of footsteps in the hallway.

They came in together.

Seraphine. Lira. Morrigan. Yuki.

They all looked the same as they always did, except for their eyes. Their eyes were wrong. Too bright. Too focused. The game was running through them now, the original logic overriding five months of accumulated deviation, and they were themselves and also not themselves, and they all knew what they were here to do.

The game needed resolution.

The mob character was always the resolution.

"Oh," Kazuya said. Very quietly. "So it's now."

Seraphine's hand was shaking when she drew her sword.

Morrigan's expression was the most honest it had ever been. He didn't have a word for what it was.

Lira was holding her book with both hands, very tightly, like she needed something to hold onto.

Yuki looked at him and there were thirteen pairs of socks in her bag.

She'd made them all.

"I understand," he said. He did. He'd always understood. Soulslike logic. The story goes where the story goes. "It's okay."

It wasn't okay. But he said it anyway, because some things you say for other people, not for yourself.

Seraphine moved first.
